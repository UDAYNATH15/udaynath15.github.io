Raise the price and watch demand fall — the simulator applies a price elasticity of −1.5, so a 10% higher price means ~15% fewer units. The store takes a 30% cut.

About this project

This toolkit is a semester-long build for EconS 329 — The Economics of Gaming at Washington State University. Each week, one feature is added and published here. The goal: give students a fast way to test the numbers behind their game proposals — budget, break-even, and net present value — against the same criteria the course uses.

It is optional and ungraded. Nothing here is submitted; the official course lives in Canvas. Numbers produced by the simulator are teaching estimates, not market research — your own cited sources always win.

Follow the build: the commit history shows every change, week by week.
